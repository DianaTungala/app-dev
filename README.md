# Friday the 13th (1980)

> *"Camp Blood is having its new guests... and they're going to die one by one."*

**Friday the 13th** is a landmark 1980 American independent slasher film directed and co-produced by Sean S. Cunningham, and written by Victor Miller. It is widely regarded as one of the most influential horror films of all time and spawned a massive, generation-spanning franchise.

---

## 🎬 Quick Facts

* **Release Date:** May 9, 1980 (United States)
* **Director:** Sean S. Cunningham
* **Writer:** Victor Miller
* **Music:** Harry Manfredini
* **Box Office:** $59.8 million on a $550,000 budget

---

## 🏕️ Plot Overview

The story is set at **Camp Crystal Lake**, a summer camp that has been closed down for decades following a series of tragic events—including the drowning of a young boy named **Jason Voorhees** in 1957 and subsequent mysterious murders. 

Despite local warnings about a "death curse," a group of young counselors arrives to fix up and reopen the campgrounds. As a severe thunderstorm cuts them off from the outside world, an unseen, relentless killer begins stalking and brutally murdering the counselors one by one.

---

## 🎭 Main Characters

* **Alice Hardy** (Played by Adrienne King) – The resourceful and resilient final girl of the film.
* **Pamela Voorhees** (Played by Betsy Palmer) – The grieving mother seeking vengeance for her son's tragic death.
* **Steve Christy** (Played by Peter Brouwer) – The optimistic owner determined to reopen Camp Crystal Lake.
* **Bill, Brenda, Ned, Jack, Marcie, and Annie** – The group of ill-fated camp counselors.

---

## 🌟 Legacy & Cultural Impact

1. **The Iconic Antagonist:** Though Jason Voorhees is the face of the franchise, fans are often surprised to learn that his mother, Mrs. Voorhees, is the primary killer in the first film.
2. **The Hockey Mask:** Jason did not wear his signature hockey mask until *Friday the 13th Part III* (1982).
3. **Soundtrack:** Harry Manfredini’s iconic **"Ki-ki-ki, ma-ma-ma"** vocal sound effects (derived from "Kill, her, mommy") have become shorthand for cinematic suspense and terror.
